Measured from the water edge in the recent record this plot clears the buffer.
Measured from the edge in the earliest record it does not. The water body has
retreated here, and the distance between the two edges is reported.

This is the finding the tool exists to make. A setback drawn from today's
shoreline inherits whatever encroachment moved that shoreline, so a plot can
satisfy the buffer as measured and still sit inside the buffer as it would have
been drawn from the original boundary. Which boundary governs is a question for
the notified record, and the referral asks for exactly that.
